MRO Spare Parts Management:
Best Practices That Actually Hold

Less downtime, less capital tied up in the storeroom. A practical method for safety stock, ABC classification, turnover and predictive maintenance — plus what to do when a part is discontinued.

Principle 01

Set safety stock from real variability, not habit

Most storerooms hold too much of what never fails and too little of what does. Safety stock should be derived from demand variability and supplier lead-time variability, not from a rule of thumb.

Safety stock = Z × √(LT) × σ_d   +   Z × d × σ_LT

Where Z is the service-level factor (1.65 ≈ 95%, 2.33 ≈ 99%), LT is lead time, σ_d is the standard deviation of demand, d is average demand and σ_LT is lead-time variability. If you cannot yet measure σ, start with service level by criticality and refine as data accumulates.

Reorder point = (average demand × lead time) + safety stock
Imported parts often have long and variable lead times. When σ_LT is high, consolidating shipments or holding a larger single batch is usually cheaper than repeated expedited freight.
Principle 02

Classify with ABC, then act differently per class

ABC classification ranks items by annual consumption value so control effort is spent where the money is. Many teams add a second axis — criticality — because a cheap sensor can stop an expensive line.

ClassShare of itemsShare of valueControl approach
A~10–20%~70–80%Tight control, frequent review, accurate records, negotiated contracts
B~20–30%~15–20%Periodic review, moderate safety stock
C~50–70%~5–10%Simplified control, bulk ordering, higher buffer

For criticality, mark items that stop production as critical spares regardless of value — they warrant availability over cost optimisation.

Principle 03

Measure turnover, then remove the dead stock

Inventory turnover exposes capital sitting still. Low turnover with high availability usually means obsolete or over-specified stock.

Turnover = cost of spares consumed per year ÷ average spare-parts inventory value

Review items with zero consumption over 24 months. Common causes: equipment already decommissioned, part numbers superseded, or duplicates recorded under different codes. Cleaning up part-number duplication often releases more cash than any renegotiation.

Principle 04

Move from reactive to predictive maintenance

Predictive maintenance replaces calendar-based replacement with condition-based action, so parts are changed when they need it rather than when the schedule says so.

  • Vibration analysis — bearings, gearboxes, motors; detects imbalance and misalignment early
  • Thermography — electrical cabinets, connections, overheating bearings
  • Oil analysis — wear metals and contamination in hydraulic and lubrication systems
  • Run-hour and cycle counters — simplest reliable trigger for consumable wear parts

The practical gain is planning: a predicted failure becomes a scheduled job with the part already in stock, instead of an unplanned stop with an expedite fee.

Principle 05

When the part is discontinued

Obsolescence is the hardest MRO problem: the OEM no longer supplies the item and the line still needs it. A structured cross-reference is usually faster and cheaper than a redesign.

  • Capture the full nameplate — model, rating, mounting, connector type, tolerance class
  • Separate functional requirements from brand requirements; many parts are interchangeable across makers
  • Map against equivalent domestic and imported codes, then confirm with a sample trial
  • If no equivalent exists, assess a form-fit-function replacement or a small custom build
We maintain a cross-reference covering hydraulics, pneumatics, sensors, drives and bearings. Send the nameplate data or the old part number and we will propose verified alternates — browse the spare-parts cross-reference.
Principle 06

Digitise only after the data is clean

MRO software amplifies whatever data you give it. Before implementing a system, ensure each physical item has one code, one description and one location. Duplicate part numbers are the most common reason MRO systems fail to deliver savings.

Once the master data is trustworthy, a system can add reorder alerts, min/max warnings and spend analysis — the point at which MRO Pro becomes useful rather than decorative.
