What is custom automation

Why build, not buy

Custom (non-standard) automation means equipment engineered to your specific product shape, process and cycle time—not a standard machine pulled from a catalog. Any line with repetitive manual assembly, inspection or loading steps can gain consistency, yield and capacity through purpose-built automation, while reducing dependence on skilled labor.

FAQ

Common questions

What is custom (non-standard) automation?

Custom automation means equipment engineered to a buyer's specific product shape, process and throughput, rather than bought off-the-shelf. Typical deliverables include automated assembly lines, dedicated jigs and fixtures, gauges, loading/unloading mechanisms and line retrofits. The value is standardizing manual work, raising speed and cutting defect rates.

Why source custom automation from China?

China offers a dense mechanical and electronic supply chain, mature CNC and sheet-metal capacity, and competitive labor for machining and assembly—translating into lower unit cost and shorter lead times than building the same line domestically in high-cost regions. Zhuao combines that supply-chain edge with engineering disciplines (CAE, vision inspection, controls) expected by international buyers.

What is a typical custom automation delivery process?

Six stages: requirement & capacity analysis, concept design & mechanism selection, CAE simulation and prototype validation, machining and assembly, on-site installation/commissioning and trial production, then after-sales maintenance and spare parts. Zhuao confirms milestones with the buyer at each stage so delivery fits the real line rhythm.

Which industries are a good fit for custom automation?

Packaging, aerospace, electronics, medical devices, chemical, printing, logistics and warehousing, and defense—any industry with specific requirements for precision, cycle time or cleanliness. Any line with repetitive manual assembly, inspection or loading steps can gain consistency and capacity through custom automation.

How are cost and lead time controlled on a custom project?

Define the requirement boundary and acceptance criteria early, and reuse proven mechanism modules wherever possible. Modular design, standardized pneumatic/transmission components and clear stage milestones shorten manufacturing time and lower per-unit cost while keeping reliability.
